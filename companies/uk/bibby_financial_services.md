# Company / Lender Teardown — Bibby Financial Services

**Status:** Batch 3 first-pass teardown  
**Research date:** 2026-10-02  
**Geography:** United Kingdom / international context  
**Primary segment:** S6 — invoice finance / factoring  
**Primary question:** how does SME credit change when the risk object is receivables/debtors rather than a conventional amortizing loan?

> Quality standard: `docs/consulting_quality_standard.md`

---

## Executive synthesis — answer first

**Bibby closes a major gap in the research: invoice finance is not just another SME loan product. The financed asset is the receivable/sales ledger, liquidity scales with invoicing, and repayment comes from debtor collections rather than a fixed amortization schedule.** That changes underwriting, monitoring, customer journey, funding and risk.

### Quantified proof points

Current scale:
- **8,500+ businesses** funded;
- operations across **9 countries**;
- about **£6bn** of customer turnover managed annually;
- invoice factoring can release up to **85%** of invoice value, usually within **24 hours**;
- July 2026 HSBC facility: **€250m**;
- total available funding capacity: **>£1.1bn** across Europe/Asia;
- 2024 Bibby Financial Services turnover: **£188.6m**;
- 2024 BFS PBT: **£11.2m**.
- 60% of new-business prospects in 2025 opted to add Bad Debt Protection.

### Mechanism

B2B invoice created
→ receivable becomes financeable collateral
→ funder advances cash
→ debtor pays
→ facility is repaid / remaining balance released
→ funding availability grows with sales ledger.

### Strategic implication

Receivables finance should be benchmarked separately from term loans because:
- risk object differs;
- monitoring data differ;
- collections are embedded;
- availability is tied to invoices;
- customer value proposition is cash conversion, not only borrowing.

### Transferability

**High for B2B borrowers with verifiable receivables.**

### Counter-evidence

The model is not suitable for all SMEs:
- requires B2B invoicing;
- debtor quality matters;
- operational integration / credit-control processes can be more intensive than a simple term loan.

## 1. Product model

### Invoice Factoring

Public description:
- up to **85%** of invoice value;
- usually within 24 hours of raising/setting up invoices;
- BFS handles credit control / collections;
- remaining balance paid after debtor settles, net of fees;
- no fixed loan repayment schedule.

### Invoice Discounting

Similar receivables-backed liquidity, but customer often retains more control over collections.

### Bad Debt Protection

Optional protection against customer insolvency/default.

This is structurally important because the product can combine:
- financing;
- debtor-risk transfer;
- collections outsourcing.

---

## 2. Customer journey

### Factoring

1. SME establishes facility.
2. B2B invoice is raised.
3. Invoice/sales ledger data are submitted/integrated.
4. BFS advances up to 85%.
5. BFS performs credit-control/collection under factoring arrangement.
6. Debtor pays.
7. Remaining invoice value released minus fees.
8. New invoices replenish funding availability.

The recurring journey is operational rather than a one-time application.

## 4. Underwriting / risk architecture

Risk depends on:
- SME sales ledger;
- invoice validity;
- debtor/customer quality;
- dilution/disputes;
- concentration;
- payment terms;
- industry;
- historic collections.

In factoring, BFS can also control collections, changing monitoring and recovery economics.

### Bad-debt signal

BFS 2026 SME research:
- average unpaid invoices: £66,770;
- 30% of SMEs had written off bad debt;
- affected companies wrote off nearly £30k on average;
- 60% of new-business prospects in 2025 chose Bad Debt Protection.

This is demand-side context, not BFS portfolio loss data.

## 5. Funding model

BFS is a non-bank funder.

Current public funding:
- 2025 securitisation facility approximately **£0.7bn**;
- July 2026 HSBC UK facility **€250m**;
- total available funding capacity **>£1.1bn**.

The HSBC structure is described as **back-to-back receivables financing**.

This closely matches asset-liability structure:
receivables-backed SME assets funded by institutional/bank facilities.

## 7. Hypothesis tests

### H5 — SME is several businesses: very strongly supported
Receivables finance is a fundamentally different credit operating model.

### H3 — funding architecture: strongly supported
Asset-backed institutional facilities match receivables funding.

### H4 — data substitution: relevant but different
Invoice/sales-ledger data become the core underwriting/monitoring object.

### H2 — speed is segmentation: supported
Once facility is established, individual invoice liquidity can be rapid because the credit framework already exists.

---

## 8. Strategic implication

For a bank, invoice finance should not be treated as a “feature” next to a term loan.

It requires a distinct factory:
- invoice ingestion;
- debtor limits;
- concentration monitoring;
- collections;
- reconciliation;
- fraud controls;
- funding against receivable assets.

But it can solve working capital more naturally than unsecured borrowing for B2B sellers.
