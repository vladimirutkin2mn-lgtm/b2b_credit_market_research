# Company / Lender Teardown — Judo Bank

**Status:** Batch 2 first-pass teardown  
**Research date:** 2026-10-02  
**Geography:** Australia  
**Primary segments:** S4/S5 — larger SME relationship lending  
**Primary question:** can a deliberately human, high-touch SME bank generate attractive economics without mass-market automation?

> Quality standard: `docs/consulting_quality_standard.md`

---

## Executive synthesis — answer first

**Judo is the strongest counterexample in the project to the assumption that the optimal SME model is maximum automation.** It deliberately prices for bespoke service and banker judgment, serves larger/secured SME exposures, and has scaled to A$14.7bn of lending while improving NIM and cost efficiency. But FY26 also shows the cost of this model's credit concentration: impairment charges rose sharply and 90+ DPD/impaired assets increased.

### Quantified proof points — FY26

- gross loans/advances: **A$14.7bn**, +18%;
- SME lending customers: **4,822**;
- deposits: **A$12.2bn**, +24%;
- deposits = **71% of total funding**;
- NIM: **3.13%**, +20bps;
- cost-to-income: **45.3%**, improved 710bps;
- PBT: **A$168.1m**, +34%;
- ROE: **6.4%**, up from 5.3%;
- impairment expense: **A$117.7m**, up 56%;
- impairment / avg GLA: **88bps** vs 66bps;
- 90+ DPD & impaired: **2.90%** vs 2.43%;
- blended lending margin: **4.2% over 1m BBSW**;
- average performing-portfolio PD: **2.08%**;
- management's through-cycle write-off framework: ~**50bps** based on ~2% PD / ~25% LGD.

### Mechanism

Experienced relationship banker
+ direct decision-maker access
+ bespoke assessment and collateral
→ higher service/speed for non-standard SME cases
→ higher lending margin.

Scale + deposit funding + operating leverage
→ rising profitability.

But bespoke larger secured exposures
→ idiosyncratic concentration/default risk
→ specific provisions can be volatile.

### Strategic implication

There is a profitable role for **human underwriting when the ticket and complexity are high enough to pay for it**. The target should not be zero humans; it should be high banker productivity and decision authority.

### Transferability

**Transferable mainly to medium/upper-SME segments.**

---

## 1. Position and customer

Judo is a dedicated SME bank rather than a universal retail bank.

FY26:
- 4,822 SME lending customers;
- A$14.7bn loan book.

That implies a rough average gross exposure of ~**A$3.0m per lending customer**. This is a derived diagnostic, not a reported average loan ticket and can include multiple facilities/customer.

This puts Judo structurally far away from instant micro-SME lenders.

---

## 2. Underwriting operating model

Judo's disclosed process centers on:
- relationship banker;
- direct customer meeting;
- income/financial position;
- transaction statements;
- bureau;
- tax/ATO data;
- valuations/security;
- credit-risk control;
- in-market decision authority.

Historic/current disclosure emphasizes that relationship bankers and credit executives can make judgments without multiple central “head office sign-off” layers.

This is **human judgment redesigned for speed**, rather than automation replacing judgment.

---

## 3. Products

Judo supports:
- business loans;
- lines of credit;
- asset finance;
- bank guarantees;
- commercial/business secured financing;
- SME-customer home lending.

Portfolio is predominantly secured; FY26 materials indicate the vast majority of credit is fully/partially secured.

### Pricing architecture

Judo's 2026 investor presentation explicitly maps lending margin against borrower risk grade.

At Jun-2026:
- blended lending margin: **4.2%** over 1-month BBSW;
- average performing-portfolio PD: **2.08%**.

This is unusually transparent evidence of risk-based pricing.

---

## 4. Customer journey

The public business-lending journey is relationship-led:

1. broker/direct referral;
2. relationship banker contact;
3. meeting / understand business;
4. borrower supplies financial/transaction/security information;
5. banker + credit executive assess;
6. tailored pricing/terms;
7. legal/security documentation;
8. settlement;
9. ongoing banker relationship.

### Visual customer journey

Judo does not expose a self-serve SME application screenflow comparable with fintechs because the product is deliberately relationship-led.

**Screen evidence missing by design / public availability.**

This is not necessarily a UX weakness for the target segment; the key experience variable is access to empowered decision-makers.

---

## 5. Economics

FY26:
- income: A$522.4m;
- OPEX: A$236.6m;
- pre-provision profit: A$285.8m;
- impairment: A$117.7m;
- PBT: A$168.1m;
- NPAT: A$111.1m;
- NIM: 3.13%;
- CTI: 45.3%.

This is strong evidence of operating leverage:
revenue grew faster than expenses.

### Funding

Deposits:
- A$12.2bn;
- 71% of total funding;
- increasing share.

Judo also uses wholesale funding and completed a A$750m capital-relief securitisation.

Deposit growth improved funding efficiency and supported NIM.

---

## 6. Risk

FY26 deterioration:
- 90+ DPD/impaired: 2.90%, +47bps;
- impairment expense: A$117.7m, +56%;
- customer write-offs: A$87.3m vs A$38.7m FY25.

Management cites:
- individually assessed problem credits;
- macro overlays;
- pressured sectors.

This is the important counterweight to the relationship-banking narrative: bespoke judgment does not eliminate concentration losses.

---

## 7. Hypothesis tests

### H5 — SME is several businesses: very strongly supported
Judo's average exposure and relationship process are categorically different from micro-SME digital credit.

### H3 — funding advantage: supported
Deposit share is rising and NIM improved as funding mix improved.

### H2 — speed is segmentation: supported
Speed is achieved through empowered humans, not straight-through automation.

### H4 — data substitution: not central
Data helps underwriting, but the model's proposition is judgment/service rather than removing documents.

---

## 8. Strategic implication

For medium/upper SME, a strong target model may be:

**digital preparation + empowered relationship banker + short approval chain**

rather than:
**fully automated underwriting**.

The economic test is whether larger ticket and margin cover banker/OPEX/capital while losses remain controlled.

---

## 9. Counter-evidence

FY26 risk deterioration proves that:
- banker experience is not a substitute for portfolio controls;
- secured credit can still create meaningful specific loss;
- faster in-market judgment needs concentration/vintage discipline.

---

## 10. Evidence gaps

1. Median/average facility size (not customer exposure).
2. Banker productivity by origination.
3. Approval time distribution.
4. Customer journey screens / CRM tooling.
5. New-vs-repeat economics.
6. Industry vintage loss curves.
7. RAROC by sector/product.
