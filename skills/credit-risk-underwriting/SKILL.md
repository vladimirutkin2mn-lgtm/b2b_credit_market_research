# SME Credit Risk & Underwriting

## Objective

Describe how risk is selected, priced, approved and monitored — and how that shows up in portfolio performance.

Use EBA loan-origination principles, Basel credit-risk concepts, OCC lending guidance and company disclosures as anchors.

## Step 1 — Borrower segmentation

Capture:
- legal form;
- revenue / sales band;
- employee count if relevant;
- firm age;
- industry;
- geography;
- existing vs new customer;
- owner/guarantor dependency;
- secured vs unsecured;
- product / loan size / tenor.

Do not assume one underwriting model applies across all SME segments.

## Step 2 — Information used in underwriting

Map observable inputs:
- financial statements;
- management accounts;
- cash-flow / bank transaction data;
- tax data;
- accounting integrations;
- bureau / trade-credit data;
- owner/guarantor bureau;
- business model / purpose;
- financial projections;
- collateral;
- guarantees;
- existing relationship behavior;
- invoices / receivables;
- merchant/acquiring data;
- legal / registry data.

Mark each as required / optional / inferred / unknown.

## Step 3 — Decision architecture

Determine where possible:
- fully automated;
- rules + model;
- analyst review;
- relationship manager input;
- credit committee;
- manual exception path;
- approval authority by amount/risk grade.

Separate:
- eligibility;
- pre-approval;
- conditional approval;
- final credit approval;
- booked/disbursed loan.

## Step 4 — Core credit logic

Assess whether evidence shows use of:
- repayment capacity / cash flow;
- leverage;
- debt service coverage;
- liquidity;
- profitability;
- account behavior;
- collateral coverage;
- guarantor support;
- sector risk;
- concentration;
- fraud/KYB;
- stress / sensitivity.

Never invent score weights or cutoff rules.

## Step 5 — Terms as risk controls

Capture:
- amount;
- tenor;
- amortization;
- interest rate / risk-based pricing;
- collateral;
- guarantees;
- covenants;
- information undertakings;
- line utilization limits;
- renewal/review frequency.

Underwriting is not only approve/decline; structure and terms are part of risk selection.

## Step 6 — Monitoring

Look for:
- periodic financial refresh;
- transaction monitoring;
- covenant monitoring;
- bureau refresh;
- limit review;
- early-warning indicators;
- delinquency triggers;
- restructures;
- watchlist / problem credit;
- collections and recovery.

Distinguish application-time data from ongoing monitoring.

## Step 7 — Portfolio-quality metrics

Collect with definitions:
- delinquency buckets;
- default rate;
- NPL/NPE;
- Stage 2 / Stage 3, where IFRS applies;
- allowance / ECL;
- charge-offs;
- recoveries;
- cost of risk;
- vintage loss curves;
- approval rate;
- booked conversion;
- repeat borrower performance.

Never treat approval rate as a risk metric without applicant mix.

## Step 8 — Vintage / mix analysis

Check whether changes in loss metrics are driven by:
- seasoning;
- product mix;
- loan size;
- risk grade;
- new vs existing customer;
- macro conditions;
- underwriting changes;
- collections changes;
- portfolio acquisitions/sales.

## Step 9 — Risk-adjusted product comparison

For each product compare:
- expected return;
- expected loss;
- collateral/guarantee;
- duration;
- capital usage;
- operational intensity;
- fraud exposure;
- early-warning observability.

Avoid “lower NPL = better underwriting” without controlling for mix and seasoning.

## Output

1. Borrower segments.
2. Underwriting data map.
3. Decision flow.
4. Credit criteria evidenced.
5. Risk mitigants / terms.
6. Monitoring lifecycle.
7. Portfolio metrics.
8. Vintages/mix.
9. Unknowns and proprietary areas.
10. Confidence.

## Hard rules

- EBA/Basel/OCC are methodological anchors, not proof that a specific lender follows a particular process.
- Do not infer model variables from public marketing language.
- Do not compare default, delinquency, Stage 3 and NPL as if equivalent.
- Always retain the original source definition and denominator.
