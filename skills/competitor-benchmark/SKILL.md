# SME Lender Competitor Benchmark

## Objective

Create a comparable, source-grounded view of lenders without confusing company marketing, borrower experience and financial performance.

## Step 1 — Define peer set

Classify each player:
- universal bank;
- SME-focused bank;
- digital bank / neobank;
- fintech balance-sheet lender;
- marketplace / broker;
- embedded lender;
- factoring / invoice finance;
- leasing / asset finance;
- government-backed / specialist lender.

State whether the comparison is like-for-like.

## Step 2 — Use identical research lanes

For every competitor collect the same fields.

### A. Scale and economics
- active business customers;
- SME borrowers, if disclosed;
- outstanding loan book;
- originations;
- revenue / net interest income attributable to lending if available;
- yield / NIM where disclosed;
- cost of risk / provisions;
- NPL/default/delinquency;
- funding model;
- RWA / capital intensity where relevant;
- profitability or contribution margin where disclosed.

### B. Product
- term loan;
- revolving line / overdraft;
- business card;
- invoice finance / factoring;
- asset/equipment finance;
- real-estate secured business loan;
- merchant cash advance / revenue-based finance;
- guarantees / government-supported loans.

For each: amount, term, pricing, fees, collateral, guarantee, repayment, early repayment and eligibility.

### C. Acquisition and eligibility
- existing-customer only vs open market;
- channels;
- pre-qualification / pre-approved offers;
- required tenure / revenue / entity age;
- industries excluded;
- guarantor requirements.

### D. Underwriting
- financial statements;
- bank transaction data / open banking;
- accounting integrations;
- tax data;
- credit bureaus;
- owner/guarantor data;
- collateral;
- manual vs automated decisioning;
- stated decision SLA.

Do not infer proprietary model weights unless directly evidenced.

### E. Customer journey
- entry point;
- authentication;
- eligibility check;
- application fields;
- documents/data connections;
- consent;
- decision;
- offer comparison;
- signing;
- disbursement;
- servicing;
- renewal/top-up/refinance;
- collections / hardship where visible.

### F. Servicing capabilities
- drawdown;
- repayment schedule;
- autopay;
- statements;
- payoff;
- extra payment;
- covenant/document upload;
- limit increase;
- repeat borrowing;
- support channels.

## Step 3 — Source each field

Preferred:
1. filings / Pillar 3;
2. official product pages, pricing, terms, help center;
3. regulator / official surveys;
4. app evidence and external customer evidence.

Mark every field:
- Verified;
- Inferred;
- Unknown.

Unknown is valid.

## Step 4 — Normalize

Before side-by-side comparison normalize:
- currency;
- period;
- loan amount basis;
- APR / nominal rate / flat fee;
- secured vs unsecured;
- borrower size;
- new vs existing customer;
- stock vs flow;
- gross vs net loss metric.

## Step 5 — Identify structural differences

Explain *why* offers differ:
- funding cost;
- risk appetite;
- distribution model;
- relationship banking;
- automation;
- collateral;
- guarantee schemes;
- regulatory capital;
- cross-sell economics.

Avoid subjective “best/worst” scoring unless a user supplies a specific operational criterion; prefer factual trade-offs.

## Output

1. Peer definitions.
2. Normalized comparison matrix.
3. Per-player one-page summary.
4. Distinctive capabilities.
5. Structural economics/risk differences.
6. Journey friction differences.
7. Evidence gaps.
8. Changes over time, if enough historical data.

## Hard rules

- A fast advertised decision time is not equal to time-to-cash.
- “Up to” limit is not typical loan size.
- Representative pricing is not portfolio yield.
- Product eligibility is not approval probability.
- Reviews identify friction hypotheses, not prevalence.
