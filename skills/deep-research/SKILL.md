# Deep Research

## Objective

Turn a question into an auditable chain:

**question → hypotheses → evidence plan → sources → claims → contradictions → synthesis → confidence**

The output must make it easy to distinguish what is known, calculated, inferred, and still unknown.

## Step 1 — Frame the question

Write:
- decision/question;
- geography;
- period;
- SME definition;
- product scope;
- 2–5 falsifiable hypotheses;
- what evidence would disconfirm each hypothesis.

Do not start broad web collection before these are explicit.

## Step 2 — Build the source plan

Use a source hierarchy:

1. regulators / official statistics;
2. audited company filings / Pillar 3;
3. official product, pricing, terms, help and developer pages;
4. reputable industry / academic research;
5. reputable media;
6. reviews, forums, social and other anecdotal evidence.

Use lower tiers mainly for discovery, UX friction and hypotheses; do not let them override stronger direct evidence without explanation.

## Step 3 — Fan out by evidence lane

For each question collect independently across relevant lanes:

- market statistics;
- company financial disclosures;
- credit-risk disclosures;
- product/pricing;
- application/servicing process;
- external customer evidence;
- regulation / definitions.

Avoid repeatedly searching the same type of source.

## Step 4 — Record claims, not just links

For every material claim capture:

- claim text;
- source;
- publication date and data period;
- geography;
- exact definition / denominator;
- evidence type: fact / calculation / estimate / hypothesis;
- confidence;
- caveats;
- conflicting evidence.

A list of URLs is not research.

## Step 5 — Triangulate

High-impact claims should ideally have:
- one primary/direct source; and
- one independent cross-check.

Use different evidence types when possible.

If two independent methods disagree materially:
1. do not average automatically;
2. compare definitions;
3. compare data periods;
4. compare coverage (banks vs all lenders, SME vs micro only, stock vs flow);
5. identify the most likely reason for divergence;
6. report a range when the disagreement cannot be resolved.

## Step 6 — Adversarial review

Before synthesis ask:

1. What evidence most strongly contradicts our current conclusion?
2. Are we confusing correlation, company messaging or accounting presentation with economics?
3. Is there a denominator/definition change that breaks the comparison?
4. What would a skeptical credit officer, CFO and product leader each challenge?
5. Which conclusion depends on one weak source?

Search specifically for disconfirming evidence.

## Step 7 — Confidence

Use:
- **High** — direct primary evidence + independent cross-check, definitions aligned.
- **Medium** — good evidence but incomplete comparability or one key assumption.
- **Low** — proxy, anecdotal, stale, inferred or materially conflicting evidence.

Do not convert Low to High by adding more low-quality sources.

## Step 8 — Synthesis format

For each major question produce:

### Finding
One concise conclusion.

### Evidence
Key facts with source/date/definition.

### Calculation
If applicable, show formula and assumptions.

### Counter-evidence / uncertainty
What could make the finding wrong.

### Confidence
High / Medium / Low.

### Next evidence needed
The highest-value missing data.

## Hard rules

- Never invent unavailable fields.
- Never infer customer emotions without evidence.
- Never use one company's marketing copy as proof of actual SLA/performance.
- Never compare percentages without checking denominator.
- Never mix stock, flow, applications, approvals and disbursements.
- Never hide a source conflict.
- Never use “latest” without checking publication and data dates.
- Separate company-wide figures from SME-lending figures.
