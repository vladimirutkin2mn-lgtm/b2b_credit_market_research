# SME Lending Economics

## Objective

Explain how a lender makes money, where losses arise, and which economics are structural versus accounting presentation.

## Core P&L bridge

A simplified lending contribution view:

`Interest + fees - funding cost - expected credit loss - operating cost - fraud/ops losses = pre-capital contribution`

Then consider:
- capital consumption / RWA;
- taxes;
- shared overhead;
- cross-sell value;
- deposit value and transfer pricing where relevant.

Do not claim a “true unit economics” number unless the required inputs are actually disclosed.

## Step 1 — Map reported financials

Collect:
- average loan book;
- gross interest income;
- funding / interest expense;
- fee income;
- net interest income;
- impairment / provision expense;
- charge-offs / write-offs;
- operating expenses;
- segment profit;
- capital / RWA.

Document whether data are:
- company-wide;
- business banking segment;
- SME-specific;
- product-specific.

Never silently allocate company totals to SME lending.

## Step 2 — Pricing and yield

Distinguish:
- advertised borrower rate;
- APR/effective rate;
- fees;
- portfolio gross yield;
- interest income / average earning assets;
- net interest margin.

A product price is not a portfolio yield.

## Step 3 — Funding economics

Identify:
- deposits;
- wholesale funding;
- securitization;
- warehouse lines;
- equity / own balance sheet;
- partner-bank model.

If possible estimate:

`Gross spread ≈ portfolio yield - marginal/average funding cost`

State clearly which funding-cost concept is used.

## Step 4 — Credit cost

Track separately:
- provision / impairment expense;
- charge-off;
- recoveries;
- NPL/default/delinquency;
- ECL / ACL allowance;
- Stage 1/2/3 or CECL measures where applicable.

Useful ratios when definitions permit:
- cost of risk = impairment charge / average loans;
- net charge-off rate = net charge-offs / average loans;
- NPL ratio = nonperforming loans / gross loans;
- coverage = allowance / NPL or relevant impaired exposure.

Do not mix these into one “loss rate”.

## Step 5 — Operating cost

Look for:
- acquisition;
- sales / relationship manager;
- underwriting;
- KYC/KYB/AML;
- document handling;
- servicing;
- collections;
- customer support;
- technology / data;
- fraud / manual review.

Where direct cost is unavailable, use process proxies only as clearly labeled estimates.

## Step 6 — Unit economics

Possible borrower/loan-level framework:

`Expected lifetime revenue`
minus
`funding`
minus
`expected loss`
minus
`acquisition + underwriting + servicing + collections`
minus
`capital charge`
=
`risk-adjusted contribution`

For revolving products account for:
- utilization;
- unused commitment;
- line management;
- repeated draws.

For short-duration products use annualized metrics carefully.

## Step 7 — Risk-adjusted return

Where enough information exists, estimate:
- ROA on lending assets;
- RAROC / return on allocated capital;
- contribution per booked loan;
- contribution per active borrower;
- payback period on acquisition/underwriting cost.

Show formula, allocation assumptions and sensitivity.

## Step 8 — Cohort/vintage economics

Whenever possible analyze by:
- origination vintage;
- risk grade;
- loan size;
- term;
- channel;
- secured/unsecured;
- new-to-bank vs existing;
- repeat vs first loan.

Portfolio averages can hide deteriorating new vintages.

## Step 9 — Cross-sell

For universal banks, SME credit may support:
- current accounts / deposits;
- acquiring/payments;
- payroll;
- FX;
- cards;
- cash management;
- insurance.

Credit standalone profitability and relationship profitability are different questions.

## Output

1. Reported financial map.
2. Normalized lending P&L.
3. Yield and funding.
4. Credit cost.
5. OPEX/cost-to-serve.
6. Capital intensity.
7. Unit economics / RAROC if defensible.
8. Cohort evidence.
9. Cross-sell economics.
10. Sensitivity and missing data.

## Hard rules

- Do not back-solve precision from segment disclosures that do not support it.
- Provision expense is not the same as realized credit loss.
- NIM is not borrower pricing.
- ROE can rise because leverage/capital allocation changed.
- Always state denominator and period for every ratio.
