# Executive Synthesis — SME Lending Operating Models

**Version:** 2026-10-02  
**Evidence base:** 43-player universe, 18 company deep dives, 8 markets  
**Audience:** CEO / CRO / CFO / Head of SME / Product / Strategy

---

# Executive answer

The market does **not** converge toward one “best SME lender” or one universal digital-credit model.

The strongest evidence instead points to a different conclusion:

> **Winning SME lenders match five things to one another: customer need, observable data, decision model, funding architecture and risk/capital intensity.**

Fast journeys, strong economics and acceptable credit quality emerge when those five elements fit the segment.

The practical implication is that SME lending should be designed as a **portfolio of credit factories**, not one process for every business borrower.

Across the 18 deep dives, the evidence supports 12 major conclusions.

---

# 1. SME lending is several businesses, not one market

## Answer

The most important segmentation variable is not the legal SME definition.

The operating model changes materially by:
- ticket;
- credit need;
- security;
- risk object;
- data availability;
- relationship depth.

## Evidence

Observed models include:
- transaction-visible micro liquidity;
- digital unsecured working capital;
- government-guaranteed credit;
- asset/capex finance;
- medium/upper-SME relationship lending;
- receivables finance;
- embedded merchant credit.

A €30k cash-flow loan, a A$3m relationship facility and invoice factoring should not be benchmarked as one product.

## Mechanism

Different risk objects require different:
- data;
- underwriting depth;
- servicing;
- collections;
- funding;
- capital.

## Implication

Build segment-specific factories, metrics and customer journeys.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 2. The best digital journey often starts before the application

## Answer

The shortest visible journeys occur when lenders continuously underwrite customers before an explicit borrowing request.

## Evidence

Examples:
- Nubank: Capital de Giro appears after recurring automated analysis.
- Stone: pre-approved offer is pushed from merchant relationship/revenue data.
- CommBank: eligible existing customers can receive conditional approval based on information already held.
- Mercado Pago: seller activity creates eligibility and the customer begins from an available offer.
- Square: merchant accounts are reviewed continuously for loan eligibility.
- Amex: select existing customers can see pre-approval / line information before a full application.

## Mechanism

Ongoing relationship
→ data accumulate
→ credit eligibility updates continuously
→ customer enters at offer/configuration stage.

## Implication

For incumbent banks, “existing SME customer” should be a distinct credit product architecture, not merely the same application with prefilled fields.

## Counter-evidence

This advantage weakens for:
- new-to-bank customers;
- sparse transaction history;
- complex/larger exposures.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 3. Data substitution matters more than form simplification

## Answer

The strongest UX improvements come from replacing borrower-entered evidence with machine-readable evidence.

## Evidence

Observed substitutions:

| Traditional evidence | Machine-readable alternative |
|---|---|
| bank statements | Open Banking / transaction feed |
| revenue declaration | acquiring / marketplace sales |
| annual accounts for smaller tickets | cash-flow history |
| manual turnover proof | tax / e-invoice data |
| repeated KYC/business questions | existing relationship data |
| repeated borrower documents | prior repayment + behavior |

Examples:
- Rabobank: 13 months transaction data can replace annual accounts inside a defined route.
- Floryn: six months bank data can replace annual accounts up to €250k.
- Konfío: SAT/CIEC/invoice data underpin underwriting.
- SBI: GST, UPI, ITR and bank data support digital SME lanes.
- iwoca: Open Banking simplifies smaller-ticket underwriting.

## Implication

A digital-lending roadmap should prioritize **data integrations and policy redesign before front-end redesign**.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 4. “Instant” lending is usually a bounded risk lane

## Answer

The evidence does not support universal straight-through processing across SME.

Fast lanes exist inside explicit risk perimeters.

## Evidence

- iwoca: document requirements increase above €50k.
- Rabobank: no-annual-accounts route capped at €250k / 5 years and subject to transaction/sector rules.
- SBI Digi Sugam: digital path has defined amount and data conditions; larger enhancement redirects to branch.
- CommBank: instant path strongest for eligible existing customers.
- Stone: automated and dedicated desks operate differently.
- Commerzbank: ≤€100k can be decided in adviser consultation; larger cases move into deeper process.

## Mechanism

Lower exposure + strong data + standardized product
→ automation.

Higher exposure / weaker data / collateral / complexity
→ more information + human judgment.

## Implication

Design an **automation ladder** with explicit escalation rules.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 5. Human underwriting is not inherently legacy

## Answer

For larger SME exposures, human judgment can be economically rational and strategically differentiating.

## Evidence

- Judo: A$14.7bn loan book across 4,822 lending customers; relationship-bank model; NIM 3.13%.
- Allica: digital decision-in-principle followed by explicit underwriter review; £3.7bn lending and profitable growth.
- Commerzbank: digital intake quickly routes to an empowered adviser.
- SBI: automated smaller-ticket products coexist with large SME-processing / relationship infrastructure.

## Mechanism

Larger ticket
→ higher gross economics and risk at stake
→ banker/underwriter cost becomes affordable
→ bespoke structure may improve pricing and risk selection.

## Implication

The target is not “remove humans.”

It is:
- automate administration;
- surface the right data;
- reduce handoffs;
- give qualified decision-makers authority.

## Counter-evidence

Human judgment does not eliminate losses. Judo's FY26 impairments increased materially despite a relationship-led model.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 6. Fast digital lending does not automatically create superior credit risk

## Answer

Better data improves observability and process design, but does not remove underwriting mistakes, adverse selection or vintage deterioration.

## Evidence

Stone combines:
- merchant transaction data;
- embedded distribution;
- automated repayment;
- proactive offers.

Yet Q2 2026 disclosed:
- >90-day NPL of 8.6%;
- cost of risk of 21.5%;
- weaker prior vintages and dedicated-desk cases contributing to deterioration.

Judo also shows that human underwriting can experience specific-loss volatility.

## Implication

Judge digital underwriting on:
- vintage losses;
- approval mix;
- cohort profitability;
- repeat performance;

not on decision speed alone.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 7. Funding advantage matters — but deposits are only one solution

## Answer

Sustainable SME economics require either attractive funding or an asset-light way to transfer/recycle funding and credit exposure.

## Evidence

### Deposit-funded
- Itaú;
- SBI;
- Rabobank;
- CommBank;
- Judo;
- Allica.

### Institutional / capital-light
- Funding Circle Term Loans: institutional forward-flow capital; H1 2026 Term Loan PBT margin 26.4%.
- Square: majority of Square Loans sold to third-party investors.

### Securitisation / wholesale
- Floryn: €150m private securitisation facility.
- Bibby: receivables-backed funding capacity >£1.1bn.
- UGRO: wholesale-funded NBFC with 10.16% cost of borrowings.

## Implication

Funding architecture should be selected **product by product**, not institution-wide.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 8. High borrower yield is not the same as attractive economics

## Answer

Customer pricing cannot be evaluated without funding, losses, operating cost and capital.

## Evidence

UGRO FY26:
- portfolio yield: 17.50%;
- cost of borrowings: 10.16%;
- GNPA: 2.50%;
- material credit cost and OPEX;
- reported ROA: 2.1%.

Funding Circle shows another contrast:
- mature capital-light Term Loans profitable;
- faster-growing FlexiPay/Card still loss-making at H1 2026 and carrying ECL/funding burden.

## Implication

Use a normalized economic bridge:

**yield/fees  
− funding  
− credit loss  
− OPEX  
− capital cost  
+ cross-sell/relationship value  
= risk-adjusted contribution**

## Confidence

**High.**

---

# 9. Existing-customer lending can create a structural economic flywheel

## Answer

The relationship advantage affects more than speed.

It can potentially improve:
- CAC;
- data quality;
- repeat conversion;
- servicing;
- cross-sell;
- deposit/payments economics.

## Evidence

- Amex: existing relationship can affect preapproval, pricing and available line; repeat draw avoids full reapplication.
- SBI: PABL/PAsBL use existing account/UPI behavior.
- Nubank: account usage feeds recurring eligibility.
- Stone/Square/Mercado Pago: transaction relationship creates both distribution and underwriting data.
- CommBank: ~90% of business loans linked to a transaction account.

## Implication

For universal banks/platforms, the correct unit of economics may be the **customer relationship**, not the individual loan.

## Unresolved

Public disclosures rarely quantify:
- existing-vs-new loss;
- CAC difference;
- repeat-loan contribution.

## Confidence

**High on mechanism; Medium on quantified economic uplift.**

---

# 10. Government guarantees are a distinct credit operating model

## Answer

Guarantees change risk and capital economics enough that they should not be mixed with ordinary unsecured SME lending.

## Evidence

- Itaú: government-sponsored SME originations +47.3% QoQ in 2Q26; digital FGI contracting expanded rapidly.
- Stone: government-backed lines described as lower-risk and requiring lower provision coverage.
- SBI: guarantee/program architecture is embedded in MSME lending.

## Mechanism

Government guarantee
→ lower uncovered lender loss
→ potentially less collateral
→ different approval/risk appetite
→ different provisioning/capital economics.

## Implication

Benchmark:
- approval;
- pricing;
- loss;
- RWA/capital

separately for guaranteed and ordinary credit.

## Confidence

**High on mechanism; Medium on comparable economics.**

# 11. “Bank vs fintech” is a weak strategic taxonomy

## Answer

The operating model is better explained by four dimensions:

1. **Risk object**
2. **Data source**
3. **Decision mechanism**
4. **Funding model**

## Examples

### Risk object
- borrower cash flow;
- merchant transaction stream;
- receivable/debtor;
- collateral/asset;
- guaranteed exposure.

### Data
- bank transactions;
- Open Banking;
- tax/e-invoice;
- acquiring;
- marketplace;
- financial statements.

### Decision
- pre-approved automated;
- new-to-lender STP;
- rules + analyst;
- empowered RM;
- committee.

### Funding
- deposits;
- wholesale;
- institutional forward flow;
- securitisation;
- receivables facility.

## Implication

Competitive benchmarking should compare **factories**, not labels.

A bank and fintech may operate more similarly than two products inside the same bank.

## Confidence

**High.**

# 12. The strongest strategy is modular: automate where risk is standardized, preserve judgment where complexity pays for it

## Answer

The combined evidence supports a modular target operating model rather than one end-state.

### Lane A — Pre-approved micro / existing customer
Data:
transactions / acquiring / platform behavior.

Decision:
continuous automated eligibility.

Examples:
Nubank, Square, Stone, Mercado Pago, SBI PABL.

### Lane B — Digital small-business cash-flow credit
Data:
Open Banking / tax / accounting.

Decision:
automation + exceptions.

Examples:
iwoca, Floryn, Konfío, Funding Circle.

### Lane C — Guarantee-backed SME
Data:
ordinary underwriting + program eligibility.

Decision:
bank risk + guarantee framework.

Examples:
Itaú, Stone, SBI.

### Lane D — Medium/upper-SME relationship
Data:
financials + relationship + collateral.

Decision:
empowered underwriter/RM.

Examples:
Judo, Allica, Commerzbank.

### Lane E — Receivables / asset-backed
Data:
invoice/debtor or collateral data.

Decision:
specialist credit factory.

Examples:
Bibby, Allica asset/property products.

## Implication

A leading SME lender should not try to force all clients through one process.

It should build a **common data/risk platform with multiple origination factories**.

## Confidence

**High.**

# What is likely transferable to another bank

## High-transferability patterns

### 1. Separate existing-customer and new-to-bank journeys
Prerequisite: transaction relationship.

### 2. Introduce explicit ticket/data automation thresholds
Prerequisite: risk/policy redesign.

### 3. Replace manual statements with machine-readable data
Prerequisite: Open Banking / tax / acquiring / accounting access.

### 4. Push decision authority closer to customer for medium SME
Prerequisite: skilled credit staff + governance.

### 5. Treat repeat borrowing as continuous re-underwriting
Prerequisite: behavior/repayment data.

### 6. Separate guaranteed lending economically
Prerequisite: public/private guarantee schemes.

---

# What is highly context-specific

### Marketplace/acquiring credit
Requires:
- platform scale;
- payments data;
- integrated servicing.

### Tax-data underwriting
Requires:
- reliable digital fiscal/e-invoice infrastructure.

### Pure relationship-bank model
Requires:
- sufficiently large ticket/margin;
- skilled bankers;
- deposit/funding economics.

### Receivables finance
Requires:
- B2B invoices;
- debtor risk infrastructure;
- operational collections/reconciliation capability.

---

# What remains unproven

The research does **not yet** support a robust cross-company conclusion on:

1. highest RAROC model;
2. exact automation cost advantage;
3. loss advantage of existing-customer lending;
4. repeat-loan uplift in contribution;
5. standardized guarantee capital benefit;
6. best universal ticket breakpoint;
7. best single underwriting dataset.

These should remain open questions rather than be converted into recommendations.

# Recommended target-state design for an incumbent SME bank

The evidence suggests a target architecture with:

## One common data layer
- transactions;
- bureau;
- tax;
- accounting;
- acquiring;
- invoices;
- collateral/registry;
- customer history.

## Multiple credit factories
1. pre-approved existing-customer;
2. automated small-business cash flow;
3. guarantee-backed;
4. asset/receivables;
5. relationship SME.

## One economics framework
Measure each lane on:
- yield/fees;
- funding;
- credit cost;
- OPEX;
- capital;
- cross-sell;
- repeat value.

## One customer principle
**Never ask the customer for data the institution already has or can retrieve reliably.**

---

# Final current conclusion

The strongest lenders in the sample are not characterized by one technology, one funding source or one UX pattern.

They are characterized by **fit**.

> **The data source must fit the risk object.  
> The decision model must fit the ticket and complexity.  
> The funding must fit the asset.  
> The customer journey must reflect what the lender already knows.**

That is the emerging strategic architecture of high-quality SME lending.

**Overall confidence:** High on operating-model conclusions; Medium on cross-company risk-adjusted economics because public disclosures are not sufficiently normalized to rank models on RAROC.
