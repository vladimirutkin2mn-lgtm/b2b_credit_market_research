# Borrower × Need × Product Segmentation

Version date: 2026-10-02.

## Objective

Define the operating-model segments that actually compete for the same SME credit need.

We do **not** assume that legal SME size alone determines the lending model.

The working unit is:

**Borrower context × credit need × ticket/duration × observable data × risk structure × product**

This segmentation is a hypothesis framework and will be refined as the player universe grows.

---

# Segment S1 — Transaction-visible micro / very small business liquidity

## Typical borrower
- micro or very small business;
- often owner-managed;
- short operating history can be acceptable;
- meaningful transaction history exists with a bank, payment provider, marketplace or account platform;
- financing need is relatively small and time-sensitive.

## Core needs
- inventory;
- payroll;
- supplier payment;
- short cash-flow gap;
- emergency liquidity;
- small equipment purchase.

## Typical products
- pre-approved working-capital loan;
- small revolving line / overdraft;
- business card;
- merchant/revenue-linked loan;
- invoice-level pay-later.

## Underwriting model
High potential for automation because the lender can observe:
- bank-account transactions;
- acquiring/payment volume;
- marketplace sales;
- account tenure;
- repayment behavior;
- bureau / registry data.

## Customer journey
Expected best-in-class pattern:
1. offer appears in existing app/account;
2. borrower selects amount;
3. sees price/fee and repayment;
4. signs digitally;
5. funds arrive quickly;
6. repayment is automatic or tightly integrated.

## Evidence signals

### United States
In the Fed 2025 SBCS, among financing applicants:
- 21% sought ≤$25k;
- 16% sought $25k–$50k.

Therefore **37% sought no more than $50k**.

Source:
https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey

### Germany
KfW reports that **67% of new SME investment loans were €50k or less** in 2024, while average investment-loan borrowing for micro firms with fewer than five employees was roughly **€32k**.

Source:
https://www.kfw.de/PDF/Download-Center/Konzernthemen/Research/PDF-Dokumente-KfW-Mittelstandspanel/PDF-Dateien-Mittelstandspanel-%28EN%29/KfW-Mittelstandspanel-2025_EN.pdf

### India
SBI reports pre-approved business loans up to **₹20 lakh** based on transaction history, and automated BRE processing for broader SME loans up to ₹5 crore using bureau, GST, ITR and banking data.

Source:
https://sbi.co.in/documents/17826/35696/Annual%2BReport%2BFY2025.pdf/1b6a1b79-a16d-b9d1-d28f-be8d7edf60d5

## Hypothesis
This is the segment where **existing-customer data advantage** should create the largest improvement in speed and underwriting cost.

# Segment S2 — Small established business: digital unsecured working capital

## Typical borrower
- established small company;
- recurring revenue;
- may be new-to-lender;
- limited hard collateral;
- can provide bank statements / open banking / tax / accounting data.

## Core needs
- general working capital;
- project pre-financing;
- inventory;
- hiring;
- marketing;
- seasonal liquidity.

## Typical products
- unsecured term loan;
- flexible term loan;
- revolving line;
- overdraft.

## Underwriting model
- bank transactions / open banking;
- bureau;
- financials where ticket grows;
- tax/GST/accounting data depending market;
- automated decision for lower ticket;
- manual exception or analyst review above threshold.

## Evidence signals

### Germany — iwoca
Current public offer:
- €1k–€500k;
- paperless application in ~5 minutes;
- decisions up to €15k can often be automated immediately;
- otherwise decision within 48 hours;
- for €1k–€50k, business account transactions can be supplied by Open Banking;
- above €50k, additional BWA / accounting documents are required.

Source:
https://www.iwoca.de/geschaeftskredit
https://www.iwoca.de/finanzierung

This is a useful observed threshold where process/data requirements change with ticket size.

### India — Lendingkart / Indifi
Digital NBFC/fintech models advertise:
- collateral-free credit;
- online applications;
- bureau, GST, bank-statement and KYC data;
- short fulfillment timelines.

Sources:
https://www.lendingkart.com/business-loan/check-loan-eligibility
https://www.indifi.com/

## Hypothesis
This is the main battleground between:
- incumbent-bank digital lending;
- fintech balance-sheet lenders;
- partner/marketplace models.

# Segment S3 — Guarantee-backed small-business credit

## Typical borrower
- micro/small/medium business that may lack sufficient collateral;
- otherwise bankable cash flow;
- eligible under a government guarantee programme.

## Core needs
- working capital;
- investment;
- expansion.

## Products
- guarantee-backed term loan;
- guarantee-backed working-capital facility.

## Structural enabler
Government/third-party guarantee reduces lender loss exposure or collateral requirement.

## Evidence signals

### Brazil
Stone offers FGI PEAC and other guarantee-supported products with digital contracting. FGI PEAC eligibility extends to firms with annual revenue up to R$300m.

Source:
https://www.stone.com.br/credito/linhas-incentivadas

Itaú offers:
- Pronampe for firms up to R$4.8m revenue;
- FGI PEAC up to R$300m revenue;
- digital simulation/contracting for eligible customers.

Sources:
https://www.itau.com.br/empresas/emprestimos-financiamentos/fgo
https://www.itau.com.br/empresas/emprestimos-financiamentos/fgi

### India
CGTMSE and other guarantee schemes are structurally important in MSME lending and are embedded in bank product architecture.

## Hypothesis
Guarantees can shift approval/collateral economics, but should be treated as a distinct product/risk model rather than blended with ordinary unsecured lending.

# Segment S4 — Capex / equipment / asset-backed SME finance

## Typical borrower
- established small or medium enterprise;
- identifiable long-lived investment;
- borrowing duration longer than a short cash-flow gap.

## Core needs
- machinery;
- vehicles;
- IT equipment;
- premises;
- expansion capex.

## Products
- term investment loan;
- equipment finance;
- leasing;
- commercial mortgage;
- secured term loan.

## Underwriting
More emphasis on:
- project economics;
- financial statements;
- asset value/security;
- repayment capacity;
- firm history.

## Evidence signals

Germany provides strong evidence that investment finance behaves differently from generic working-capital lending:
- €81bn bank borrowing was used by SMEs to finance investment in 2024;
- 615k SMEs used bank loans for investment;
- average borrowing was €131k but highly skewed by business size.

KfW source:
https://www.kfw.de/About-KfW/KfW-Research/KfW-Mittelstandspanel.html

## Hypothesis
Automation can simplify intake and document collection, but full straight-through underwriting should decrease as ticket, tenor and asset complexity rise.

# Segment S5 — Medium / upper-SME relationship lending

## Typical borrower
- medium or upper-SME;
- larger ticket;
- multiple products;
- more complex ownership/cash flows;
- may require covenants, collateral or guarantees.

## Core needs
- expansion;
- acquisition;
- commercial property;
- large working-capital lines;
- multi-product financing;
- trade finance.

## Products
- revolving facilities;
- larger term loans;
- secured lines;
- commercial mortgages;
- trade finance;
- guarantees;
- bespoke structures.

## Operating model
- relationship manager;
- analyst/credit officer;
- financial statements;
- business plan/projections where needed;
- collateral;
- committee/approval authorities.

## Evidence signals

### Germany
KfW's average investment borrowing rises sharply with firm size, while micro firms are much smaller-ticket. This is direct evidence that “SME” contains materially different credit operating models.

### India
SBI's FY2025 model combines:
- automated BRE for SME facilities up to ₹5 crore;
- Asset Management Teams / SME centres;
- relationship managers for higher-value relationships.

Source:
https://sbi.co.in/documents/17826/35696/Annual%2BReport%2BFY2025.pdf/1b6a1b79-a16d-b9d1-d28f-be8d7edf60d5

### UK
Allica's public application model requests bank statements, company/management accounts and debt information, then uses underwriter review for established businesses.

## Hypothesis
For this segment, “best digital journey” means **reducing document and coordination friction**, not necessarily instant approval.

# Segment S6 — Receivables / trade / supply-chain finance

## Typical borrower
- B2B seller;
- meaningful receivables;
- longer customer payment terms;
- possibly tied to large corporate buyers.

## Core needs
- bridge invoice-to-cash gap;
- supplier financing;
- export/import;
- order/project pre-financing.

## Products
- invoice finance;
- factoring;
- receivables-backed line;
- supply-chain finance;
- trade credit / LC / BG.

## Underwriting object
Risk can be partially shifted from the SME itself to:
- invoice;
- debtor;
- corporate anchor;
- receivable pool.

## Hypothesis
This segment should be analyzed separately from unsecured SME loans because the risk object, monitoring and funding mechanics differ.

# Segment S7 — Embedded / acquiring / marketplace merchant credit

## Typical borrower
- merchant or seller already operating on a payment/acquiring/marketplace platform;
- lender has proprietary high-frequency revenue data.

## Core needs
- inventory;
- marketing;
- short growth capital;
- working capital.

## Products
- merchant cash advance;
- revenue-linked loan;
- pre-approved term loan;
- embedded line.

## Underwriting / servicing advantage
- near-real-time sales data;
- customer tenure;
- transaction frequency;
- chargeback/refund behavior;
- automated repayment from sales.

## Evidence signals

### US — Square
Square advertises customized financing offers based partly on processing history and repayment as a percentage of daily sales.

### US — PayPal
PayPal Working Capital uses PayPal sales history and revenue-linked repayment.

### Brazil — Stone
Stone says offers consider relationship, monthly revenue and market history. Eligible clients receive a pre-approved offer in the app, and repayment can be retained automatically from sales.

Sources:
https://www.stone.com.br/capital-de-giro
https://ajuda.stone.com.br/pt_BR/capital-de-giro/capital-de-giro-stone

## Hypothesis
This is one of the cleanest environments to test whether proprietary transaction data can simultaneously improve:
- acquisition cost;
- underwriting speed;
- monitoring;
- collections;
- repeat lending.

# Segment boundaries are not universal ticket bands

We deliberately **do not set one global USD/EUR ticket boundary** for “micro”, “small” and “medium” lending.

Why:
- firm definitions differ by country;
- purchasing power differs;
- products differ;
- collateral materially changes process;
- existing-customer data can change the operating model even at the same ticket.

Instead, the player-level research should record **process breakpoints**, for example:
- where automated decision stops;
- where financial statements become mandatory;
- where RM/analyst enters;
- where collateral becomes standard;
- where approval authority changes.

These breakpoints are more useful than an arbitrary universal loan-size taxonomy.

# Cross-segment comparison framework

| Segment | Typical speed potential | Manual intensity | Proprietary-data value | Collateral relevance | Typical servicing model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 Transaction-visible micro | Very high | Low | Very high | Low | In-app automated |
| S2 Digital unsecured small | High | Low/medium | High | Low | Digital/self-service |
| S3 Guarantee-backed | Medium/high | Medium | Medium | Guarantee substitutes partly | Digital + programme controls |
| S4 Capex/asset | Medium | Medium/high | Medium | High | Digital + specialist |
| S5 Medium relationship | Lower | High | Medium/high | Medium/high | RM + digital |
| S6 Receivables/trade | High/medium | Medium | High | Receivable/anchor is key | Integrated/operational |
| S7 Embedded merchant | Very high | Low | Very high | Low | Platform-integrated |

These are working hypotheses, not performance ratings.

# Implications for company shortlisting

The 12–18 deep dives must cover **operating-model segments**, not only countries.

Minimum desired representation:

- 2–3 S1/S2 digital small-business lenders;
- 2–3 S5 relationship/incumbent models;
- 2+ S7 embedded/payment-data lenders;
- 2+ guarantee-backed examples;
- 1–2 receivables/trade specialists;
- 1–2 capex/asset-finance models.

A lender can represent multiple segments.

---

# Implications for customer journey research

We will not compare one generic “SME loan journey”.

At minimum, screen-based journeys should later include:

### Journey A
Existing transaction-visible micro/small customer with a pre-approved offer.

### Journey B
New-to-lender small business seeking an unsecured working-capital loan.

### Journey C
Established medium SME seeking a larger term/working-capital facility.

### Journey D
Merchant receiving embedded/revenue-linked financing.

### Journey E
Guarantee-backed small-business loan where programme eligibility adds steps.

This makes screen comparisons economically meaningful rather than merely visual.
