# Risk & Economics Benchmark — Source-Safe SME Lending

**Version:** 2026-10-02  
**Scope:** selected metrics from 18 deep dives  
**Purpose:** compare economic mechanisms without creating false rankings from incompatible disclosure scopes.

---

# Executive answer

Public disclosure quality is too uneven to rank 18 lenders on one RAROC table.

But the evidence is strong enough to compare **economic mechanisms**:

1. deposit-funded balance sheet;
2. wholesale/high-yield balance sheet;
3. capital-light originate-and-distribute;
4. relationship-bank premium-margin model;
5. embedded merchant credit;
6. guarantee-backed risk sharing;
7. receivables-backed finance.

The most important conclusion is:

> **SME lending profitability is not explained by yield, funding, loss or automation alone. The relevant unit is the whole economic bridge.**

---

# 1. Required economics bridge

For every product/lane:

**Borrower yield / fees**  
− **funding cost**  
− **expected / realized credit loss**  
− **acquisition cost**  
− **underwriting / KYB cost**  
− **servicing / collections cost**  
− **capital charge**  
+ **cross-sell / deposit / payments value**  
= **risk-adjusted contribution**

Only some lenders publicly disclose enough lines to approach this bridge.

---

# 2. Disclosure scope matters

Use three labels:

## A. SME/product specific
Best evidence.

Examples:
- SBI SME yield;
- UGRO portfolio yield/funding/GNPA;
- Judo dedicated SME-bank economics;
- Funding Circle Term Loan/FlexiPay segment PBT;
- Stone consolidated credit metrics.

## B. Business/segment context
Useful but broader than target product.

Examples:
- CommBank Business Banking;
- Commerzbank Corporate Clients;
- Allica whole specialist-bank economics.

## C. Group context only
Do not attribute to SME.

Examples:
- Nubank group NIM/NPL;
- Itaú group ROE/cost of credit;
- Mercado Libre group credit metrics.

---

# 3. Economic archetype A — Capital-light origination

## Funding Circle Term Loans

H1 2026:
- originations: **£1.050bn**
- AUM: **£2.953bn**
- PBT: **£28.6m**
- PBT margin: **26.4%**
- institutional forward-flow commitments: **£2.4bn**

### Mechanism

Originate / underwrite / service
→ institutional investor funds or owns much of exposure
→ lender earns origination/servicing economics
→ lower direct funding/capital burden.

### Counterpoint

FlexiPay/Card:
- £640m transactions;
- £300m AUM;
- **£4.5m H1 loss**;
- warehouse funding and ECL remain material.

### Conclusion

The same company can have:
- capital-light profitable term lending;
- balance-sheet-intense revolving lending.

**Economic architecture is product-specific.**

---

# 4. Economic archetype B — Wholesale-funded high-yield specialist

## UGRO Capital

FY26:
- portfolio yield: **17.50%**
- cost of borrowings: **10.16%**
- diagnostic yield–funding gap: **7.34ppt**
- GNPA: **2.50%**
- Embedded Finance GNPA: **1.70%**
- Emerging Market LAP GNPA: **1.20%**
- credit cost: **₹209.4cr**
- OPEX: **₹613.9cr**
- PAT: **₹174.8cr**
- reported ROA: **2.1%**

### What this proves

High asset yield can be substantially absorbed by:
- expensive funding;
- credit loss;
- operating cost;
- capital.

### What it does not prove

The 7.34ppt difference is **not NIM** and not RAROC.

# 5. Economic archetype C — Premium relationship SME bank

## Judo Bank

FY26:
- gross loans: **A$14.7bn**
- deposits: **A$12.2bn**
- deposits: **71% of funding**
- NIM: **3.13%**
- blended lending margin: **4.2% over 1m BBSW**
- CTI: **45.3%**
- PBT: **A$168.1m**
- ROE: **6.4%**
- impairment / avg GLA: **88bps**
- 90+ DPD/impaired: **2.90%**
- average performing portfolio PD: **2.08%**

### Mechanism

Larger tickets + relationship service + bespoke structure
→ premium lending margin
→ banker cost economically supportable.

### Counter-evidence

FY26 impairments rose materially and 90+/impaired increased.

Human judgment creates differentiated service/pricing; it does not remove idiosyncratic loss.

# 6. Economic archetype D — Deposit-funded specialist SME bank

## Allica

2025:
- lending: **£3.7bn**
- deposits: **£5.7bn**
- NIM: **4.7%**
- gross revenue: **£371.3m**
- gross profit after risk: **£145.3m**
- underlying PBT: **£43.7m**

### Mechanism

Deposit funding
+ specialist SME pricing
+ digital workflow
+ human underwriting
→ profitable growth in established SME.

### Gap

Current public headline disclosure does not provide a clean comparable NPL/CoR metric for the specific product lanes used in our journey benchmark.

# 7. Economic archetype E — Large deposit-funded universal bank

## SBI — unusually useful SME-specific evidence

FY26:
- SME portfolio: about **₹612,222cr**
- disclosed SME segment yield: **9.16%**
- narrower MSME portfolio: **₹450,683cr**
- MSME GNPA: **1.18%**

### Why useful

This is among the better public examples of combining:
- segment scale;
- yield;
- MSME risk.

### Missing economic lines

Still unavailable for a full SME RAROC bridge:
- allocated funding cost;
- SME OPEX;
- SME RWA/capital;
- SME credit cost.

---

## Itaú — strong scale, group economics only

2Q26:
- very small + small + middle-market portfolio: **R$307.4bn**
- +11.6% YoY reported
- group recurring ROE: **24.3%**
- group cost of credit / loan portfolio: **2.7%**
- CET1: **12.3%**

### Caveat

Do not attribute:
- 24.3% ROE;
- 2.7% cost of credit

directly to the SME/middle-market book.

Itaú is most useful here for:
- scale;
- digital guarantee adoption;
- structural funding context.

# 8. Economic archetype F — Embedded merchant lending

## Stone

2Q26:
- credit portfolio: **R$3.752bn**
- credit revenue: **R$348.5m**
- credit revenue: **+153% YoY**
- provision expense: **R$187.6m**
- cost of risk: **21.5%**
- NPL 15–90: **6.0%**
- NPL >90: **8.6%**
- coverage: **203.6%**
- government-backed loans: **R$334.2m**

### What this case demonstrates

Embedded transaction data:
- can improve distribution;
- improve monitoring;
- enable sales-linked repayment;

but does **not** guarantee low credit losses.

### Accounting caveat

Stone's disclosed credit revenue includes credit-card interchange under current presentation, so revenue/loan portfolio should not be treated as a pure loan yield.

---

## Square Loans

Jun-2026 Block commercial credit:
- held-for-investment gross: **$488.9m**
- allowance: **$32.3m**
- held-for-sale: **$676.8m**
- commercial delinquent/nonperforming balance described as immaterial
- majority of Square Loans sold to third-party investors.

### Mechanism

Payments data
+ embedded distribution
+ sales-linked repayment
+ asset sale
→ potentially strong combination of low acquisition friction and capital recycling.

### Gap

No current public Square-loan product contribution/RAROC.

# 9. Economic archetype G — Relationship ecosystem / dynamic limit

## American Express BLOC

Observable:
- fee structure;
- $2k–$250k line;
- reusable draws;
- instant transfer into eligible Business Checking;
- existing relationship can affect preapproval/limit.

SEC “Other loans” includes small-business lines but is not BLOC-only.

### Conclusion

Strong structural case for:
- relationship lifetime value;
- repeat lending;
- cross-sell;

but insufficient public BLOC-only P&L/risk data for a benchmark return.

# 10. Economic archetype H — Receivables finance

## Bibby Financial Services

Current/known:
- >8,500 businesses funded;
- ~£6bn customer turnover managed annually;
- up to 85% invoice advance;
- >£1.1bn funding capacity;
- 2024 BFS turnover: **£188.6m**
- 2024 PBT: **£11.2m**

### Mechanism

Receivables-backed asset
→ short/self-liquidating funding exposure
→ financing + servicing/collections economics
→ funding matched with receivables facilities.

### Gap

No clean public bridge for:
- financing yield;
- funding spread;
- debtor loss;
- service-margin;
- capital.

# 12. What we can compare defensibly

## Funding mechanism
Yes:
- deposits;
- wholesale;
- forward flow;
- securitisation;
- investor sale.

## Presence of product-level risk evidence
Yes.

## Operating leverage within one company over time
Often yes.

## Product-engine economics within same company
Sometimes:
- Funding Circle Term Loans vs FlexiPay.

## Risk across product engines with same reporting framework
Sometimes:
- UGRO Embedded vs EM LAP.

---

# 13. What we should NOT compare directly

Do not put in one ranking:
- Nubank group NIM vs Judo NIM vs Allica NIM;
- Stone cost of risk vs SBI GNPA;
- Funding Circle PBT margin vs bank ROE;
- product advertised APR vs accounting portfolio yield;
- Stage 3 vs NPL vs 90+ DPD.

Different numerator/denominator/scope would make the exhibit analytically false.

---

# 14. The most useful benchmark exhibit

Instead of a “who is best” table, use an economic-mechanism map:

| Model | Funding solution | Customer revenue engine | Main loss risk | Main OPEX | Capital intensity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capital-light term platform | institutional investors | origination + servicing | underwriting/investor performance | technology/acquisition | low–medium at originator |
| Deposit bank fast lane | deposits | interest + cross-sell | borrower default | platform + bank overhead | regulatory bank capital |
| Wholesale high-yield NBFC | wholesale | high loan yield | unsecured/SME loss | branches + tech | meaningful |
| Relationship SME bank | deposits/wholesale | spread / fees | concentration/specific credit | bankers | high |
| Embedded merchant | balance sheet / investor sale | fixed fee/interest + ecosystem | merchant downturn/vintage | relatively automated | model-dependent |
| Receivables finance | receivables facility | finance + service fees | debtor/fraud/dilution | collections/ops | asset-dependent |

# 15. Boardroom implications

## CFO / Treasury
Choose funding structure at product-engine level.

## CRO
Compare vintages and risk by:
**product × customer × channel × decision model**, not company average.

## Head of SME
Design multiple factories under one customer/data platform.

## Product
Measure customer-visible speed together with:
- manual review;
- loss;
- booked conversion;
- repeat value.

## CEO
Do not pursue “digital lending” as one transformation program.

Pursue a portfolio of segment-specific economic models.

---

# Final economics conclusion

The most important economic principle from the research is:

> **Automation only creates value when the underwriting cost saved, conversion gained or lifetime value created exceeds any change in credit loss, funding and capital consumption.**

There is no credible evidence that the fastest lender is automatically the most profitable or safest.

The strategic objective is **risk-adjusted contribution by credit factory**.
