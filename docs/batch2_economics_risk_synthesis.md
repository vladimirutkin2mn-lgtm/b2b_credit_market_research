# Batch 2 Synthesis — What Actually Drives SME Lending Economics

**Companies:** Funding Circle, Itaú, SBI, UGRO Capital, Judo Bank, American Express BLOC  
**Version:** 2026-10-02  
**Purpose:** test H3 funding advantage, connect underwriting models to risk-adjusted economics, and refine H1/H5/H6.

---

## Executive answer

**There is no single winning SME lending balance-sheet model. The economically attractive model is the one that matches product duration and risk with the right funding, operating intensity and customer relationship.**

Batch 2 reveals four viable structures:

1. **Capital-light origination/servicing** — Funding Circle Term Loans.
2. **Deposit-funded incumbent cross-sell** — Itaú, SBI, American Express relationship ecosystem.
3. **Wholesale-funded high-yield specialist** — UGRO.
4. **Premium-margin relationship bank** — Judo.

Each can work, but the constraints are different.

### Boardroom takeaway

A lender should manage SME credit using this bridge:

**gross customer yield / fee income**  
− **funding**  
− **credit loss**  
− **operating cost**  
− **capital cost**  
+ **relationship/cross-sell value**  
= **risk-adjusted economic contribution**

The biggest analytical error is to judge a model using only one line of this bridge.

# 2. Finding 1 — Funding advantage matters, but there are several ways to solve it

## Answer

Deposit funding is a powerful structural advantage, but Funding Circle demonstrates that a non-bank can avoid carrying much of the long-term credit exposure if institutional investors fund the asset.

### Deposit-funded examples

Itaú, SBI and Judo can fund SME lending from broad liability franchises.

Judo:
- deposits = 71% of funding;
- NIM improved to 3.13%;
- funding optimization is an explicit ROE lever.

### Capital-light alternative

Funding Circle's Term Loan economics are largely platform economics:
- originate;
- place/fund with institutional investors;
- earn transaction and servicing revenue.

H1-26 Term Loan PBT margin reached **26.4%**.

### Wholesale-funded specialist

UGRO:
- portfolio yield 17.50%;
- cost of borrowing 10.16%.

The 7.34ppt diagnostic gap exists **before**:
- credit cost;
- OPEX;
- capital.

Reported ROA was only 2.1%.

## So what?

“Cheap deposits” are not the only solution.

A lender can instead:
- distribute credit risk;
- securitize;
- use forward-flow capital;
- reserve balance sheet for high-frequency/revolving products.

But funding architecture must be designed alongside product architecture.

# 3. Finding 2 — High customer pricing does not imply high profitability

## Answer

UGRO is the clearest example.

17.5% portfolio yield sounds extremely attractive.

But:
- funding cost is 10.16%;
- credit cost is ₹209.4cr;
- OPEX is ₹613.9cr;
- reported ROA is 2.1%.

The economic moat therefore depends on:
- product mix;
- cheaper funding over time;
- operating leverage;
- loss control.

## So what?

Do not benchmark lenders on APR/yield alone.

A proper competitor economics comparison must normalize:
1. yield/fee;
2. funding;
3. expected/realized loss;
4. OPEX;
5. capital.

# 4. Finding 3 — Capital-light and balance-sheet products can coexist inside one lender

## Answer

Funding Circle is already a multi-engine model.

### Term Loans
- mature;
- capital-light;
- profitable;
- institutional funding;
- 26.4% H1-26 PBT margin.

### FlexiPay/Card
- revolving / high-frequency;
- warehouse funding;
- ECL reserve;
- still loss-making at H1-26 despite rapid growth.

## Mechanism

Term loan
→ easier to originate/distribute as an investment asset
→ fee/servicing economics.

Revolving line/card
→ lender must maintain committed liquidity and manage uncertain utilization
→ funding + ECL remain on balance sheet
→ greater lifetime customer value but greater economic complexity.

## So what?

A multi-product SME platform should use **different funding structures by product**, not one treasury model for everything.

# 5. Finding 4 — Relationship banking can be economically rational at larger tickets

## Answer

Judo shows that manual/human underwriting is not necessarily a legacy inefficiency.

Its loan book of A$14.7bn across 4,822 lending customers implies a multi-million-dollar customer exposure scale.

The bank earns:
- blended lending margin ~4.2% over 1m BBSW;
- NIM 3.13%.

That can economically support:
- experienced banker time;
- bespoke structuring;
- collateral analysis.

## But

FY26:
- impairment rose to A$117.7m;
- 90+/impaired increased to 2.90%.

Human judgment can create differentiated pricing/service, but it does not remove credit concentration.

## So what?

The right automation target depends on **loan economics**.

For a €30k/$30k loan, banker time may destroy unit economics.

For a A$3m relationship, banker judgment may be economically rational and even value creating.

# 6. Finding 5 — Existing-customer data can improve both UX and economics

## Answer

Batch 1 showed the UX effect; Batch 2 adds stronger monetization evidence.

### American Express
Existing relationship can influence:
- preapproval;
- available line;
- pricing;
- access to >$150k initial limits.

Once approved, customer can redraw without reapplying, and Business Checking creates instant funding.

### SBI
PABL:
- 1.11 lakh loans / ₹5,513cr;
- transaction-history-based;
- instant sanction.

PAsBL:
- uses CASA/UPI transaction data;
- creates leads before customer-led application.

### Itaú
Existing-client app journeys are increasingly used for government-backed credit.

## Mechanism

Relationship
→ lower customer acquisition cost
→ reusable KYC/data
→ faster underwriting
→ higher repeat probability
→ more products/deposits/payments
→ greater lifetime economic contribution.

## So what?

The economic unit should eventually be **customer lifetime relationship**, not loan-level contribution alone, for universal banks/platforms.

# 7. Finding 6 — Government guarantees shift the risk-return frontier

## Answer

Itaú and SBI show that government programs can change the feasible credit boundary.

A guarantee may allow:
- higher approval;
- less collateral;
- longer tenor;
- lower expected loss;
- better capital economics.

Itaú's government-sponsored SME originations were **+47.3% QoQ** in 2Q26, while digital FGI contracting had already scaled rapidly after app launch.

## So what?

Guaranteed lending should be modeled separately from:
- ordinary unsecured credit;
- secured credit.

Otherwise a competitor may appear to have superior approval/pricing when part of the economic risk is externally absorbed.

# 8. Finding 7 — Credit quality must be compared at the product-engine level

## Answer

Company-level NPL obscures the real question.

UGRO:
- EM LAP GNPA: 1.20%;
- Embedded Finance GNPA: 1.70%;
- consolidated GNPA: 2.50%.

Judo:
- relationship portfolio average metrics;
- sector/specific impairments drive volatility.

SBI:
- MSME GNPA 1.18%.

But definitions, collateral and borrower mix differ.

## So what?

The useful risk unit is:

**product × borrower × origination vintage × channel/decision model**

not “company NPL.”

This becomes a core requirement for Batch 3 and final synthesis.

# 9. Four viable economic archetypes

## A. Capital-light SME platform
**Example:** Funding Circle Term Loans

Economics:
- origination/servicing fees;
- institutional funding;
- low direct funding/capital need.

Risk:
- investor performance must remain attractive.

Best fit:
- standardized term loans with investable cash flows.

---

## B. Deposit-funded relationship/data ecosystem
**Examples:** Itaú, SBI, Amex ecosystem

Economics:
- deposits;
- cross-sell;
- payments/accounts;
- repeat lending.

Risk:
- bank retains risk/capital but gains information.

Best fit:
- existing-account customer base.

---

## C. High-yield wholesale-funded specialist
**Example:** UGRO

Economics:
- high asset yield;
- high funding cost;
- must tightly manage OPEX and losses.

Best fit:
- underserved segments where pricing power/data differentiation can compensate funding.

---

## D. Premium relationship bank
**Example:** Judo

Economics:
- larger tickets;
- higher margins;
- banker cost justified by exposure/complexity.

Risk:
- concentration/idiosyncratic credit events.

Best fit:
- medium/upper SME with bespoke needs.

# 10. Implications for strategic design

A lender choosing an SME model should answer five questions in sequence:

1. **What customer/need/ticket are we serving?**
2. **What data makes risk observable?**
3. **What funding structure fits the product?**
4. **What level of human effort can the gross margin afford?**
5. **What capital/loss profile remains after all of the above?**

Starting with the app or feature set reverses the logic.

# 11. What Batch 2 changes in our hypotheses

## H3 — Funding advantage remains important
**Refined, not rejected.**

Deposit funding is an advantage, but institutional distribution/securitisation can substitute for it.

New wording:
> Sustainable SME economics require either low-cost funding **or an asset-light mechanism that transfers much of the funding/credit burden**, unless exceptional pricing power compensates.

## H5 — SME is several businesses
**Strengthened.**

The economics now show why:
- micro automated;
- revolving;
- secured LAP;
- multi-million relationship lending

must be managed separately.

## H6 — Repeat lending
**Strengthened as mechanism, still under-quantified.**

Amex/SBI make repeat/relationship loops explicit, but cohort-level economic evidence remains limited.

# 13. Boardroom takeaway

**SME lending strategy is a matching problem, not a single-model race.**

The winning combination is:

**customer economics + data advantage + underwriting intensity + funding model + risk appetite + capital**

matched to one segment.

The first two batches together suggest:

> **Automate where data and ticket make risk standardized; use human judgment where margin and complexity justify it; and choose funding architecture product by product rather than institution-wide.**

Confidence: **High on operating/economic architecture; Medium on cross-company RAROC because capital and product-level loss disclosures remain inconsistent.**
